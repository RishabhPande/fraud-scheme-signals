## FTC consumer alert, 25 Nov 2026

- **FTC sues fashion retailer SHEIN for shipping delays:** [FTC alert](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2026/11/ftc-sues-fashion-retailer-shein-shipping-delays). The FTC says its new lawsuit alleges the retailer advertised fast processing and delivery while customers reported waits of weeks or months, some orders never arriving, and no proper notice or refund choice when shipping was late. These are the FTC's allegations in a filed suit, not findings. The alert ends with consumer steps: search for complaints about a seller, keep receipts and order confirmations, and note that credit cards carry more protection than some other payment types.
- A second FTC URL dated 25 Nov, [Buying something online? Here are some ways to protect yourself](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2026/11/buying-something-online-here-are-some-ways-protect-yourself), returned the same text as the SHEIN alert when fetched, so it is treated as the same alert.

## Typology

unauthorised firm (fifteen listed); domain-style firm names (most of the 26 Nov set); coin/mining/trading vocabulary in names; clone-firm warning updates (two); online-shopping delivery failure and non-notification of delay (FTC allegation)

## Candidate detection signals (hypotheses)

- [hypothesis] Many 26 Nov firm names are bare domains, several on the .ltd or .top suffix. Domain-registration and site-age checks may be a useful review step. The name or suffix alone shows nothing about conduct.
- [hypothesis] "Coin", "mine" and "trade" vocabulary can group names for review, but cannot establish what is offered.
- [hypothesis] Notices with no postal address, or with an address in a different country from the intended market, may justify checking other identity evidence. The omission or address does not prove anything about the firm.
- [hypothesis] For delivery-delay complaints, a missing delay notice or refund option is the specific pattern the FTC alleges. It is a review question for a seller, not a finding about any seller.

## Limits

These are authorisation and clone-firm warnings, not findings that each firm committed fraud beyond what the FCA states, and the FTC item reports allegations. The FCA cautions that contact details in warnings can be incorrect or belong to someone else. Phone numbers and email addresses in the notices are deliberately not reproduced. Signals above are review hypotheses only, not validated detection rules.
