## Scheme

Per the FCA, all four new listings are firms it lists as unauthorised: two are bare domain identities with no stated corporate details, one pairs a Scottish address with a generic "Ltd" name, and one presents a New York corporate-services identity. Per the FTC, grandkid (family emergency) scams use urgent calls - sometimes with voice clones or fake videos built from digital tools, plus social-media details to sound convincing - to push grandparents into paying by wire, crypto, payment app or gift card, with explicit instructions to keep it secret. The FTC's counters: slow down and hang up, verify through another family member, and treat any demand for those payment methods as a scam marker.

## Typology

unauthorised firm; domain-only identity; bank-styled branding; cross-border (US address, UK targeting); family emergency impersonation; AI voice clone; gift-card / crypto / wire payment demand; secrecy pressure

## Candidate detection signals (hypotheses)

- [hypothesis] Domains styled as banks or investment brands ("bank", "trade", "vix") with no stated legal entity, regulator, or address.
- [hypothesis] Urgent family-emergency calls demanding wire transfer, cryptocurrency, payment app or gift card payment, especially paired with a request to keep the payment secret.
- [hypothesis] Callers who know family details (names, towns) drawn from social media but who fail an on-the-spot verification question, or who resist the target hanging up and calling the family member back on a known number.
- [hypothesis] Voice-clone indicators: audio that resists natural back-and-forth, dodges unexpected questions, or pushes a rapid switch from the call to a payment rail.
