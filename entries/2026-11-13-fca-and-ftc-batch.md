## Scheme

Per the FCA, the nine additions are firms it lists as unauthorised; one (CheapCuva) is a clone copying the motor insurer Cuvva's identity, a quieter clone showing after the 12 Nov all-clone batch. Several names borrow trust signals - "bank", "savings", crypto and prestige-street addresses - without authorisation. Per the FTC's alert, impostors build lookalike FTC sites on non-.gov domains and pose as FTC staff; the agency says real FTC sites always end in .gov and that it has demanded removal of more than 30 imposter sites under its Impersonation Rule.

## Typology

unauthorised firm; clone firm (CheapCuva); regulator impersonation (FTC); bank/savings naming misuse; crypto branding; prestige-address dressing; progressive-publication read lag

## Candidate detection signals (hypotheses)

- [hypothesis] Lookalike domains of high-volume consumer brands (insurers, banks) appearing in fresh registrations, matched by edit distance against the FCA register.
- [hypothesis] Sites claiming regulator identities on non-.gov TLDs (.com, .org, .us, .online) while using regulator logos or names.
- [hypothesis] Firm names using restricted-sounding terms ("bank", "savings") with no corresponding authorisation record.
- [hypothesis] Prestige-street addresses (Threadneedle, Piccadilly) with no matching occupant in public tenant or postal records.
